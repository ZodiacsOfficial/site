September brings two ordinary lunations, five changes of sign, one planetary
station, and eleven exact major aspects. Much of the movement is concentrated:
three events arrive on September 10, Mercury makes five exact aspects between
September 12 and 21, and the full moon shares September 26 with two exact Sun
aspects.

## Moon calendar

The new moon is exact on September 11 at 03:26:50 UTC, at 18°26′ Virgo. The
full moon is exact on September 26 at 16:48:53 UTC, at 3°37′ Aries. Neither
lunation appears in the site's 2026 eclipse catalog. They are lunar phases,
not eclipses.

| Phase | Exact UTC | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New moon | September 11, 03:26:50 | 18°26′ Virgo |
| Full moon | September 26, 16:48:53 | 3°37′ Aries |

NASA's explanation is useful here because ordinary language sometimes treats
every dark or bright Moon as an eclipse. The Sun always illuminates half of the
Moon; the phase changes because our view of that illuminated half changes as
the Moon orbits Earth. At new moon, the illuminated side faces away from
Earth. At full moon, the Moon is opposite the Sun from our point of view and
most of the dayside faces us. An eclipse needs a closer alignment than either
phase supplies in an ordinary month.

Astrologically, the Virgo new moon is read as a beginning concerned with
method, maintenance, discernment, and useful work. The modest version of that
symbolism is the best one: choose a process that can be repeated, remove a
piece of waste, or make one duty easier to understand. A new moon does not
certify that a system will work. It offers the old symbolic language of a
starting point, and Virgo would like the starting point labeled.

The Aries full moon brings the traditional themes of initiative, identity,
friction, and the courage to act. The Sun is in Libra by then, so the axis asks
an old question: what belongs to one person, and what must be decided with
another? Full moons are traditionally read as moments of contrast or
culmination. They do not make a relationship end or a project succeed. The
useful practice is simpler: notice where individual desire and shared terms
have become impossible to confuse, then answer the actual situation.

NASA's International Observe the Moon Night falls on September 19 in 2026.
NASA chooses a date near first quarter because the Moon is available in the
afternoon and evening and shadows along the terminator make surface features
easier to see. That public observing date is separate from the exact new- and
full-moon receipts above. It is simply a good evening to look rather than ask a
calendar graphic to do all the work.

## Ingresses

Venus enters Scorpio on September 10 at 08:12:43 UTC. Traditional
astrology associates Venus with affection, pleasure, agreement, and value;
Scorpio brings depth, privacy, and the question of what is shared. The familiar
reading is more selective than sociable. A preference becomes easier to trust
when it can survive a direct question.

Mercury enters Libra later that day, at 16:21:13 UTC. Mercury describes
speech, thought, exchange, and the handling of information, while Libra is
associated with proportion and the terms between people. The symbolism favors
a fair hearing, but not endless deliberation. Listening is part of a decision,
not an exemption from making one.

The Sun enters Libra on September 23 at 00:05:40 UTC, the September
equinox point in the tropical zodiac. The astrological tradition reads Libra
season through balance, relationship, form, and reciprocity. Day and night are
an astronomical matter; whether an agreement is balanced remains a human one.

Mars enters Leo on September 28 at 02:49:00 UTC. Mars supplies pursuit,
heat, and friction; Leo adds authorship, play, pride, and the wish to be seen.
The combination can describe decisive creative effort. It can also describe a
lot of energy spent defending the performance of confidence. Make the thing
first. The entrance can wait.

Mercury enters Scorpio on September 30 at 11:44:42 UTC. The month's
conversation moves from Libra's negotiated terms toward Scorpio's private
facts. This is useful symbolism for research, candid accounting, and asking
what sits underneath an agreement. Suspicion is not research. A better
question usually has a noun in it.

## Station

Uranus stations retrograde on September 10 at 18:40:43 UTC, at 5°42′ Gemini.
It is the month's only planetary station. A station is the instant an outer
planet's apparent zodiacal direction changes from Earth's point of view; the
planet is not reversing its physical orbit.

Astrologers associate Uranus with interruption, invention, independence, and
the break from an established pattern. Gemini supplies language, transport,
learning, and networks. A retrograde station is traditionally read as a turn
toward review or internal revision. None of that makes every message unstable
or every machine rebellious on September 10. The restrained question is where
a supposedly settled way of exchanging information has already begun to
change.

September 12 contains the neatest example of why one aspect should not be
asked to explain a whole day. Mercury's trine to Pluto is exact at 15:59:18
UTC; its opposition to Neptune follows forty-two minutes later at 16:41:18
UTC. The tradition gives Mercury language and judgment, Pluto depth and
pressure, and Neptune imagination and blurred edges. A trine is read as flow;
an opposition as tension or contrast. Put together, the symbols suggest a
conversation that can uncover something and still misunderstand part of it.
The sensible response is not silence. It is a second question.

Mercury's trine to Uranus on September 14 adds surprise or invention, while
the opposition to Saturn on September 18 asks the idea to meet a limit. The
sextile to Jupiter on September 21 then gives the revised thought more range.
That sequence has a useful editorial rhythm: discover, test, enlarge. It is a
traditional reading of the pattern, not a forecast that every proposal will
pass through three tidy meetings.

Neptune's sextile to Pluto on September 16 is the month's only exact major
aspect between two slow-moving planets. Traditional astrology treats both as
collective or generational symbols: Neptune for ideals and dissolution, Pluto
for pressure and deep reorganization. The sextile is usually read as an
opportunity for cooperation. At their pace, the aspect belongs to a long
chapter rather than one Wednesday. The exact receipt marks a point in that
chapter; it does not make the chapter announce itself at breakfast.
