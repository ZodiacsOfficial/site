Ask five sites what sign September 22 is and you'll get five confident
answers that don't agree. The honest answer depends on the year. This
date sits against the Sun's crossing from [Virgo](/virgo/) into
[Libra](/libra/) — the September equinox — and the table above is the
actual record: in about seven years of every ten since 1940, the whole
day belongs to Virgo, while in the rest the crossing lands partway
through the day, so a birth late enough comes out Libra. No year hands
this entire date to Libra — but plenty of individual September 22
birthdays are Libran, decided by the hour.

## One day, two answers

The two signs meeting here run a clean handoff of theme: late Virgo
perfects the thing; early Libra perfects the relations around it. A
[Virgo Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-virgo/) at the last degrees is
discernment distilled — exact, useful, hard to fool. A day-one
[Libra Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-libra/) is the level dropped onto
the year's balance point: fairness as a first instinct, the other side
of the argument always audible. People born on this date, whichever
side of the line, tend to describe both — the critic's eye with the
diplomat's delivery, quality control with a courtesy layer.

## Bonding across the boundary

In love this blend is the improver who doesn't bruise: attentive to
what could be better, careful about how the observation lands, quick
to flag unfairness in either direction. It bonds through usefulness
and through conversation, and does best with partners who take both
seriously. At work the mix is genuinely valuable — editing plus
diplomacy, standards plus stakeholder patience, the reviewer teams
request by name.

## The verdict that waits

The shared friction: neither side wants to close the case. Virgo keeps
gathering evidence; Libra keeps hearing appeals. Decisions on this
date can enter permanent deliberation — thorough, balanced, and
unmade. The growth is a verdict habit: pick the deadline before the
analysis, and let "decided" count as a quality of its own.
