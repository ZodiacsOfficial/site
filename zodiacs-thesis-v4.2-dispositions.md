## 6. Decline-to-cite self-test (three remaining exposures)

1. **The transmission narrative still compresses contested history.** "Passed… into Latin Europe" smooths the near-disappearance of horoscopic practice in the early-medieval Latin West before Arabic retransmission. Mitigation: F1 shows hops, prose says "transmission," never "unbroken." Residual risk: a historian of astrology can still call the line too smooth.
2. **The priority claim.** "No earlier dated public registry… is known" is falsifiable by one counterexample, and a peer may produce one. Mitigation: the claim states its own defeat condition. Residual risk: being defeated in public (acceptable by design, but citers may not enjoy the ride).
3. **The exclusions are stated but not yet demonstrated.** Related-party attribution on-chain is genuinely hard; a skeptic can argue the unaffiliated/affiliated boundary is unenforceable and therefore the test remains manufacturable. Mitigation: per-observation exclusion decisions published in the evidence file. Residual risk: the methodology's credibility now rests on the quality of those published decisions — which is the correct place for it to rest, and a real exposure until a track record exists.
