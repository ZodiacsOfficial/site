# Zodiacs engine audit — 2026-09-22

Nine bounded auditors, one per dimension, each with a written remit and its own scratch directory; nine adversarial verifiers, one per dimension over every blocking and major finding, plus a second independent verifier on the single blocking finding; one completeness critic over the whole set. Every auditor and verifier was an AI running against the read-only worktree of `ZodiacsOfficial/site` at `39b7a127` (branch `claude/eager-ramanujan-razak3`, PR #556 open and green). No human has reviewed this work.

**Instruments and arbiters actually used.** Swiss Ephemeris 2.10.03 (pyswisseph) with `sepl_18.se1`/`semo_18.se1` — which are **DE441-based** (their headers say so; the committed numerics record that calls them DE431 is wrong) — and, for one leg, the JPL DE440 binary `linux_p1550p2650.440`; the raw `de440s.bsp` kernel read with jplephem and with the repo's own SPK readers; NAIF satellite kernels for planet centres; an independent pyerfa chain for the apparent-place reduction; IERS `finals2000A` for UT1−UTC; JPL Horizons (DE441) as the closest available arbiter, with the caveat that its `QUANTITIES=31` ecliptic is IAU76/80 and sits 0.04–0.4″ from the IAU 2006 frame, so it arbitrates at the 0.5″ level and no finer; and, for the first time in this project, four published NASA/Espenak event instants (two greatest eclipses, two Venus greatest transits). Two premises of the audit brief were false and are recorded: Swiss cannot read NAIF `.bsp` files (it silently answers with Moshier, return flag 260), and Horizons `QUANTITIES=31` is not a milliarcsecond arbiter. Every Swiss call in every script asserted the returned flag.

**Counts.** 119 findings: after verification 36 major (one of them blocking by the letter of the rule), 46 minor, 37 notes, 0 refuted. Of the 42 blocking/major findings verified, 23 were confirmed as written and 19 were adjusted (a number, a cause, or a severity); every one of the 43 verdicts carries `reproductionRan: true`. The 77 minor/note findings were **not** independently verified. The completeness critic then ran its own probes and found four things nobody had measured (§6). All raw reports, verdicts, scripts and cached oracle responses are under the audit scratch (appendix C); the findings ledger with verifier statuses is `ledger.md` beside this file.

## 1. The one-paragraph verdict

The shipped engine is not below Swiss Ephemeris because of its reduction, its houses or its search code — those are formula-correct, and several are better documented than Swiss's. It is below Swiss because of three things it inherits without examining: the truncated planetary and lunar series inside astronomy-engine (2–16″ on every planet in a modern year; over the site's own 1800–2200 span, sampled every ten days, the maxima are Venus 23″, Pluto 29″, the node 27″, Neptune 20″, Saturn's latitude 22″ — the honest headline for the supported span is **about 30″**, not the 15–19″ a single year shows), a 2004 Delta-T polynomial that is 6.3 s wrong today and 110 s wrong at 2100, and the host's timezone database, which in 98 zones returns another city's pre-1970 history. Beside it sits a research runtime whose apparent-place reduction matches an independent ERFA chain to 4×10⁻⁷″ and whose event search proves completeness where Swiss proves nothing — but which reaches no user, offers one operation, and covers 300 years. On positions, **parity with Swiss is the ceiling**: the research runtime, Swiss on its own files, Swiss on the DE440 binary and Horizons all agree to within the arbiters' frame conventions. The truthful route to "substantially better" is therefore not smaller residuals; it is adopting the research reduction as the shipped path, fixing time, and turning proven completeness, rigorous error bounds, integrity and provenance into properties a chart consumer actually receives — while closing the daily-use breadth gaps (houses, mean node, Lilith, Chiron, sidereal) to parity and saying plainly that asteroids, fixed stars, the eclipse suite and a 30,000-year range remain Swiss's.

## 4. The Swiss question, answered from the measurements

- **Positions**: parity is the ceiling. The alpha, Swiss on its DE441 `.se1`, Swiss on the DE440 binary and the raw kernel all sit at the same 0.384″/0.098″ from Horizons barycentres, and that residual is Horizons's frame. Alpha vs Swiss on the same DE440: 0.0025″. Nothing available can show any position "more accurate than Swiss"; the shipped engine is 18.8″/2.07″ and must first reach this ceiling.
- **Reduction**: the alpha is >1,000× closer to the IAU/ERFA chain than Swiss's own evaluation (Swiss's nutation departs from published 2000B by up to 0.0015″) — a provenance advantage below every arbiter's noise, not an accuracy advantage a user can see.
- **Time**: a real place to exceed. Swiss gives a Delta-T number within 0.4 s of IERS and no uncertainty; the shipped engine is 6.3 s wrong. An IERS-anchored table with a band, exact UTC→TT since 1972, UT1−UTC, a versioned backzone tzdb inside the engine and an explicit calendar model would put Zodiacs ahead of Swiss (which ships no timezone data at all) and of every Swiss-based product that trusts the host tzdb.
- **Angles**: with the true obliquity, the engine's sidereal time sits nearer the IAU 2006/2000A chain than Swiss's **default** long-term model outside 1850–2050 (0.19″ vs 1.9″ RAMC). The critic's caveat stands: Swiss exposes the IAU 2006 model through `swe_set_astro_models`, so this is a default-setting difference, not a capability lead, and it is conditional on DUT1 being applied or disclosed by both. Parity with provenance, not superiority.
- **Events at DE quality**: the critic bisected the alpha's Neptune longitude derivative for the 2027-12-15 station and landed **−43 s** from the Horizons barycentre fit, where Swiss is −76 s and `sky.json` +2,110 s — the first demonstration (a manual derivative, not yet a product) that DE-backed event timing on this stack matches Swiss and can carry a band Swiss never states.
- **Events**: proven completeness and typed verdicts already exceed Swiss in kind for geometric longitude crossings; the machinery reaches stations, aspects and latitude crossings at modest cost; apparent-of-date completeness needs the enclosure fix in §2.6. Eclipses, occultations and rise/set are Swiss's until built.
- **Rigor and provenance**: digest-verified packs with proven fit bounds, a byte-reproducible engine build, receipts that name conventions — Swiss has none of these; closing authenticity, per-result error bounds and receipt identity makes them verified properties.
- **Breadth**: houses (2 vs 24), bodies (no Chiron/Lilith/mean node/asteroids/stars), sidereal (0 vs 48), topocentric, eclipse suite, occultations, heliacal phenomena, and a 30,000-year range remain Swiss's. The first five are cheap to close to parity; the rest are not in this programme.
