SUMMARY: The time layer is the weakest link between the stack and Swiss Ephemeris, and two of its defects are large enough to swamp every arcsecond-level ephemeris improvement the alpha has made. First, production Delta-T is astronomy-engine's hard-wired Espenak-Meeus 2004 polynomials, which are provably stale: on 2026-01-01 they give TT-UT = 75.05 s against Swiss 68.90 s and Horizons 69.18 s, a +5.9 s error that moves the Moon 3.2-3.4 arcsec today, grows about 0.62 s/yr (8.3 s / 4.6 arcsec by 2030, 18.4 s / 10.1 arcsec by 2050) and is 29.9 s / 16.4 arcsec at 1600; the public methodology page and RESULTS.md describe the Delta-T gap as 'neither extrapolation is wrong; the number isn't known yet', which is false for the observed era, and nothing in the package or site exposes, pins or records Delta-T. Second, resolveLocalToUtc trusts the host ICU, whose tzdb build follows the post-1970 zone-merger policy without backzone: measured against the same machine's backzone TZif files, 98 of 418 zones return another city's pre-1970 offsets (87 zones with >=15 min error after 1900, 60 with >=60 min for >=30 days, about 7,270 zone-years), including Europe/Stockholm (Berlin's +120/+180 in 1947 where Sweden had +60: ASC 9.5 deg, MC 13.8 deg, Moon 33 arcmin), Europe/Amsterdam (1892-1940 off by 19-80 min), Atlantic/Reykjavik (1908-1968 off by 44-72 min), Europe/Oslo and Copenhagen, Aruba/Curacao (-4:30 vs -4:00, 1912-1942), and Antigua (1 h, 1912-1951), while the homepage FAQ promises pre-1922/pre-1970 history from ICU. Third, there is no Julian-calendar input path: Julian dates typed as-is are treated as proleptic Gregorian and displace the Sun by 9.5 deg (1500), 11.0 deg (Britain 1700) or 13.0 deg (Russia 1917), and the 1800-2199 share and MCP windows admit Julian-era Russian and Balkan births. On the positive side, the gap/fold algorithm is sound: an exhaustive scan of 42,861 ICU transitions across 418 zones (1,675,757 wall minutes, 1850-2100) found zero round-trip failures in both the SDK and site copies, no two transitions within 72 h, and every offset name parsed; the alpha's TDB-TT two-term series is within 22.0 us of ERFA over 1850-2150 as claimed, and its integer-millisecond TT arithmetic loses nothing measurable. Smaller items: UTC is fed where UT1 is meant (<=0.9 s: RAMC 13.5 arcsec, MC/ASC up to 44 arcsec at 66 deg latitude, Moon 0.5 arcsec), the 'lmt' flag is a fractional-minute heuristic that misfires both ways, angles use mean obliquity with an apparent sidereal time (ASC up to 128 arcsec at 66 deg vs true obliquity, which Swiss uses), receipts never record the tzdb/ICU version although the schema has the slot, and the adapter contract states the leap-second/Delta-T split backwards. To exceed Swiss here the stack needs an observed Delta-T table with an uncertainty band carried into results, exact UTC->TT via leap seconds plus IERS UT1-UTC, its own backzone-complete tzdb with version pinned in the receipt, and an explicit calendar model.
